What goes with what, as a map

The app already finds links between what you do and how you are: drinks → lower HRV the next day, later bedtime → less energy. They hold up against chance (the false-discovery check), but they sit in Findings as a list of sentences. Drawn as a map, you can see at a glance what touches what. Each outcome shows its own trend, so the map can say what to look at: an outcome that's slipping, and the things you do that go with it. The links below are made-up examples in your data's shape.

A · Two columnsWhat you do on the left, how you are on the right. A line is a link: green goes with better, red with worse, thicker is stronger, "+1d" means the next day. Tap a node to see only its links, in words. Clickable.
B · Pick an outcomeOne outcome at a time. Each input is a bar for how much it moves it, with its 90% range. The plainest to read and the most "lab".
C · The webEverything as a web, each node sized by how many links it has. Good on a laptop, crowded on a phone.
Where it goes
  1. Insights' landing: under every trend, "What to look at", the slipping outcome and what goes with it, with the map one tap away.
  2. Findings: the map on top of the links it draws; each link still opens its days as dots.
My pick
  1. A, with B's bars as what tapping an outcome opens.
  2. C on wide screens only, later, if you like the map.
